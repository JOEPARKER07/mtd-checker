What is Making Tax Digital for Income Tax?

Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax is HMRC's replacement for the annual Self Assessment return, for people above certain income levels. Instead of one return a year, you'll need to keep digital records throughout the year and send quarterly updates to HMRC using approved software.

It applies to self-employed people and landlords — not to income taxed through PAYE, like a regular salary. If your only income is from employment, MTD for Income Tax doesn't apply to you.

What happens if you miss a deadline?

MTD uses a points-based penalty system, separate from the existing late-payment penalties:

  • You get 1 penalty point for each missed quarterly update or return deadline.
  • If you submit quarterly, a £200 fine kicks in once you reach 4 points. If you submit annually, it's after 2 points.
  • Points expire automatically 24 months after the missed deadline, as long as you stay under the threshold.
  • 2026/27 is a "soft landing" year — no penalties for missed quarterly updates if you're newly joining, though you must still keep digital records and submit before your tax return deadline.
  • Late payment still has its own grace period: 30 days in your first year in MTD, then 15 days from year two onwards.

Source: HMRC penalty guidance — rules can change, always check the latest version.

What to do before your start date

  1. Confirm your exact date using the calculator above, and double-check against HMRC's official checker.
  2. Choose MTD-compatible software — ideally a few months before your start date so you're comfortable with it.
  3. Start keeping digital records early — you don't have to wait for your start date to begin, and it makes the transition smoother.
  4. Know your quarterly deadlines — typically falling about a month after each quarter ends.
  5. Talk to an accountant if your situation is complex (multiple businesses, partnerships, or income near a threshold).

Frequently asked questions

What counts as "qualifying income"?

Your total gross turnover from self-employment and property income combined, before expenses are deducted. If you have more than one self-employed business or rental property, all of it is added together.

What if my income changes from year to year?

HMRC checks your qualifying income for a specific earlier tax year to decide if you're brought into MTD from a specific start date. If your income later drops below the threshold, you may still need to continue with MTD until HMRC confirms otherwise — check GOV.UK guidance for the latest rules.

Do I need new software?

Yes — you'll need MTD-compatible software such as FreeAgent, QuickBooks, Xero or Coconut to keep digital records and submit quarterly updates. Many offer free trials so you can get set up before your start date.

Is this official HMRC guidance?

No. This is an independent tool to help you estimate your situation quickly. Always confirm your exact position using HMRC's official guidance before making decisions.

Does MTD affect limited companies?

No — MTD for Income Tax applies to individuals with self-employment and/or property income reported via Self Assessment. Limited companies pay Corporation Tax and are not in scope of this particular rollout.

I'm employed (PAYE) and self-employed — does my salary count?

No. MTD for Income Tax only looks at your self-employment and property qualifying income. Income already taxed through PAYE, like a salary, is not included when checking against the thresholds.

Can I join Making Tax Digital voluntarily before I'm required to?

Yes. HMRC allows eligible individuals to opt in early if they want to get used to digital record-keeping ahead of their mandatory start date. Check current eligibility on GOV.UK before signing up.

What if I'm in a partnership?

General partnerships are being brought into MTD for Income Tax on a separate, later timetable to individuals. If you're in a partnership, check HMRC's specific guidance rather than relying on the individual thresholds above.
